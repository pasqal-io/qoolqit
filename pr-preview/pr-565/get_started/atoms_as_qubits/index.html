Atoms as qubits

On this page, you will learn about:

  • How a neutral atom encodes a qubit and how a laser manipulates it
  • The Rydberg blockade: why two atoms close together cannot both be excited
  • How the distance between atoms and the strength of the laser decide whether a pair is blockaded

Computing with atoms

A neutral-atom quantum processor holds individual atoms in place with optical tweezers, at positions chosen by the user. Each atom is a qubit. Its electronic ground state \(|g\rangle\) encodes \(|0\rangle\), and a highly excited Rydberg state \(|r\rangle\) encodes \(|1\rangle\):

\[ |0\rangle \equiv |g\rangle, \qquad |1\rangle \equiv |r\rangle. \]

A laser couples the two levels. Its amplitude \(\tilde{\Omega}\) sets how fast an atom is transferred between \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\), and its detuning \(\tilde{\delta}\) is the mismatch between the laser frequency and the transition frequency; it acts as an energy shift of \(|1\rangle\), favoring excitation when positive. An isolated atom under a resonant laser (\(\tilde{\delta} = 0\)) oscillates between the two states, a Rabi oscillation, with probability of being in the Rydberg state at time \(\tilde{t}\) given by

\[ P_1(\tilde{t}) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\tilde{\Omega}\,\tilde{t}}{2}\right). \]

Throughout this page, quantities are written in QoolQit's dimensionless units. The next page, Programming a neutral-atom QPU, explains where these units come from.

Why two atoms are not two independent qubits

Two atoms that are both in the Rydberg state repel each other. The interaction energy decays quickly with their distance \(\tilde{r}\):

\[ \tilde{J} = \frac{1}{\tilde{r}^{\,6}}. \]

The laser is tuned to the transition of an isolated atom, and it still drives that transition if it is off resonance by up to about \(\tilde{\Omega}\). Exciting one of the two atoms works exactly as for an isolated atom. Once that atom is in the Rydberg state, however, its interaction shifts the transition of the other atom by \(\tilde{J}\): the laser is now out of tune by \(\tilde{J}\).

  • If \(\tilde{J} \gg \tilde{\Omega}\), the laser is too far out of tune to excite the second atom to the Rydberg state as well, and the pair holds at most one excitation. Therefore, the laser drives it between \(|00\rangle\) and the Bell state \(|\Psi^+\rangle = (|01\rangle + |10\rangle)/\sqrt{2}\), at the enhanced frequency \(\sqrt{2}\,\tilde{\Omega}\). This is the Rydberg blockade.
  • If \(\tilde{J} \ll \tilde{\Omega}\), the shift is too small to matter: each atom oscillates on its own and \(|11\rangle\) is reached.

Setting \(\tilde{J} = \tilde{\Omega}\) defines the distance that separates the two regimes.

Blockade radius

\[ \tilde{r}_b = \tilde{\Omega}^{-1/6} \]

Two atoms closer than \(\tilde{r}_b\) are blockaded: they cannot both be excited. A stronger laser shrinks the blockade radius, a weaker one extends it.

Inside the blockade radius (\(\tilde{J} \gg \tilde{\Omega}\), for instance \(\tilde{r} = 1\) with \(\tilde{\Omega} = 0.1\)), the probability of exciting both atoms stays close to zero. The pair behaves as a single two-level system, \(|00\rangle \leftrightarrow |\Psi^+\rangle\): the probability of exactly one excitation reaches one and oscillates faster than a single atom would, by the factor \(\sqrt{2}\).

Outside the blockade radius (\(\tilde{J} \ll \tilde{\Omega}\), for instance \(\tilde{r} = 2.5\) with \(\tilde{\Omega} = 0.1\)), the probability of exciting both atoms reaches one. The two atoms are independent qubits: their interaction is negligible and each oscillates on its own.

At the blockade radius (\(\tilde{J} \approx \tilde{\Omega}\)) the pair moves continuously from one regime to the other. The double excitation is only partially suppressed, and the populations no longer follow a clean oscillation: the peaks change height from one cycle to the next.

Note that the blockade radius can be crossed in two ways: moving the atoms or changing the laser amplitude. Either way, only the ratio \(\tilde{J} / \tilde{\Omega}\) matters, which is why QoolQit expresses both in the same unit.

What this means for a program

The blockade is the resource that QoolQit programs are built on: it is what makes atoms interact and behave as a single system, and it is set entirely by geometry and drive.

The positions of the atoms in a register decide which pairs are blockaded for a given \(\tilde{\Omega}\), and that is how a problem is mapped onto the hardware.
